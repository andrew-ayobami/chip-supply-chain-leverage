# Leverage profile (Claude's DRAFT, 31 Oct 2026; Andrew reviews)

**Big caveat:** this covers only the **13 rows Claude drafted for Andrew**, out of 35, and none of those scores have been reviewed yet. The EUV and ArF lithography rows (ASML) and Taiwan's leading-edge fab row are in Ayodele's set or the shared set, and **are not scored yet**. They will probably be among the strongest. So re-run this once all 35 rows and K5 are final.

## Where the leverage is strongest

- **Lithography (KrF C33, i-line C26) and e-beam mask writers (C14):** the only rows where time to replace is high, meaning the US would need more than 5 years to build its own supply. That's because the US has no lithography toolmaker. This is the one feature the past cases suggest really matters (rule 1 in `notes/step3_patterns_draft.md`).
- **Single-firm, near-total holds:** Tokyo Electron in advanced-packaging resist processing (C20, 92%) and Disco and Accretech in crystal machining (C08, 99.7%). These are the only rows with low substitutability, but the US could replace them in 2–5 years.

## Where it is weakest

- **Inputs where the US already has its own maker:** photomask inspection (KLA), SoC test (Teradyne), atomic layer deposition (Lam), HBM (Micron) and, if Intel owns IMS, e-beam mask writers. The share overstates the leverage here.
- **Lower-end tools with several alternatives:** dry stripping (C11), packaging tools (C16), wafers (C28), wafer handlers (C24). These have medium shares and medium substitutability.
- **Across the board:** the suppliers all rely heavily on the US for their security (dependence is high on all 13 rows), and their governments can direct the firms only through export licensing (state control is medium on all 13).

## Which past case it most resembles

- **By score distance,** 10 of the 13 rows are closest to **CIPEC (K2)**, which failed. Two (C08, C20) are closest to **China–Japan 2010 (K4)**, which backfired. Three (C14, C26, C33) tie between CIPEC and the **oil embargo (K1)**, which partly worked. Distance here is the number of score steps between a row and a case, added up across the six variables.
- **On the variable that separated the past cases (time to replace),** most rows score medium, like CIPEC and China–Japan, not high like the oil embargo. Only the lithography and mask-writer rows look like the one case that partly worked.
- **Reading so far:** the profile looks more like the cases where threats failed or backfired than the one that partly worked. That fits the working hypothesis, that the credible asks are conditions attached to supply, not threats to cut it. This is still a draft, built on unreviewed scores and 4 past cases.
- **How to read the closeness:** much of CIPEC's closeness comes from shared "medium" state control and "high" dependence, which don't vary across today's rows. Read it as "no stronger than CIPEC on most variables", not as a precise match.
