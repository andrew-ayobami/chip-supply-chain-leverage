# Step 3 patterns: what the past cases show

Built from the final case scores in `scoring/cases_final.csv`. The case details are in `notes/step3_cases.md`. Four cases (K6–K9) were added in November 2026 so that the set includes leverage used as a **condition**, not only cuts and price schemes. K9 is too recent to have an outcome, so the patterns below use the eight cases with known endings.

Direction: low substitutability helps the supplier, and so does low dependence on the target. For the other four variables, high helps the supplier.

## Rules of thumb

1. **Conditions did better than cuts.** Both condition cases worked (K6, K7). No cut fully worked: two partly worked and two backfired, and both price schemes failed or backfired. With two condition cases this is a pattern, not a proof.
2. **Cuts only held where the target needed more than five years to replace supply.** The two cuts that partly worked (K1 oil, K8 CoCom) both scored high on time to replace. The two that backfired (K4, K5) did not.
3. **A condition can work even when the target could get around it within a few years.** In K7 the US could have kept EU data in Europe within 2–5 years, yet it changed its intelligence rules instead. What the two condition cases share is a narrow, specific demand that the target could meet while still getting what it wanted: safeguards in exchange for uranium, redress rights in exchange for data flows. Complying cost less than going around the supplier.
4. **Supply management fails when substitutes are easy.** Copper (K2) and tin (K3) had plenty of alternatives, and neither group got a lasting hold on its market.
5. **Even leverage that works wears down.** The oil embargo led to the International Energy Agency, stockpiles and new supply. CoCom held for decades but leaked (Toshiba–Kongsberg). Conditions last longer when they become the normal terms of trade, as nuclear safeguards did.
6. **A big power will give ground on a sensitive security matter if the condition is specific and the alternative is costly.** In K7 the US changed how its signals intelligence works, which is about as sensitive as policy gets, to keep EU data flowing.

## Which variables separate the outcomes

- **Form of leverage separates them best:** conditions worked, cuts at best partly worked, price schemes failed.
- **Time to replace still separates the cuts** (high for the two that partly worked), but not the conditions (K7 worked at medium).
- **Substitutability separates the price schemes** (high, and both failed), but not the rest.
- **Cohesion now runs the right way, weakly:** the two cases that worked scored medium and high; the two cuts that backfired scored low.
- **Concentration, dependence on the target and state control don't separate the outcomes.** Both condition cases scored high on dependence, so relying on the target did not stop them working.

## Factors the variables may be missing

- **How narrow and specific the demand is, and whether the target can meet it without losing face.** This now looks like the most important missing factor (K6 and K7 against K4 and K5).
- **Whether supply keeps flowing to those who comply.** In both condition cases the target kept getting what it wanted once it accepted the terms.
- **Whether the supplier gains anyway** (oil producers earned more whatever the US did).
- **Market conditions** (tight oil in 1973, slack metals markets in the 1970s and 1980s).
- **Stockpiles and money** (US tin stockpiles; the tin council running out of borrowed money).
- **Leakage through intermediaries** (oil companies redirecting cargoes; Toshiba–Kongsberg).
- **Ambiguity over intent** (K4, where it is disputed whether China targeted Japan).

## Caveats

- **Two condition cases is very few.** In K6 the condition was also backed by a pause in supply while terms were renegotiated, so it is not a pure condition.
- **The scores were drafted by someone who knew the outcomes,** so they may lean towards fitting them.
- **K6 and K7 are about verification and legal safeguards, not chips.** They are the closest available precedents for attaching safety conditions to supply, but the lessons transfer imperfectly.
