Step 3 brief for Ayo (need-to-know version, 21 Oct 2026)

What the project asks
Which AI-safety asks could the countries that hold chip-supply-chain chokepoints (other than the US and China) credibly make of the US? To judge "credibly", we compare today's chokepoints with five past supplier coalitions whose endings we know. Step 3 scores those past cases.

Step 1: what we found
- A country is in if its firms hold 40% or more of world supply of a specific chip input, such as EUV lithography tools. Countries come from this rule, not from our picks. The US and China are left out, because they're the sides a coalition would bargain with.
- Result: 8 countries. Japan, the Netherlands, South Korea, Taiwan and Germany, plus Austria, Singapore and Sweden (small specialist toolmakers). The same 8 pass at 25% and at 60%.
- The main data is ETO's chip supply chain dataset (Sept 2026 release, mostly 2025 figures). HBM memory comes from TrendForce (2025 projection: South Korea about 81%). Leading-edge chipmaking comes from SIA/BCG (2022: Taiwan 69%).
- Caveats: the figures come from different years (2019 to 2025). Most chemicals, such as photoresists, had no share data. Advanced packaging couldn't be measured.

Step 2: how we score
- There are six variables, each scored low, medium or high: concentration, substitutability, time to replace, cohesion, dependence on the target, and state control. What each level means is in the anchors (pasted separately). Read them before scoring.
- Mind the direction. For four variables, high helps the supplier. For substitutability and dependence on the target, low helps the supplier. Score what's true, not what helps.
- Cohesion is counted, not judged: how many separate actors must hold the line. 1 is high, 2 to 3 is medium, 4 or more is low.
- Score each case as it stood at the START of the episode, not from how it ended. The outcome is recorded separately, and it's what the scores are meant to explain.
