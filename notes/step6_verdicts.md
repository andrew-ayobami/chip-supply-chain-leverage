# Step 6: credibility, and the final sort

## Credibility rule (set before judging any ask)

Credibility asks whether the coalition could actually get the ask. It is scored low, medium or high:

- **high:** the US would plausibly accept on its own terms, because the ask costs it little or helps it. No leverage is needed.
- **medium:** the US government or US firms would resist, but the ask can be backed by a **condition** on supply rather than a cut. It also lines up with at least part of US policy (Congress, agencies or firms), so the coalition would be pushing with an existing US current, not against it.
- **low:** it needs a **threat to cut supply** or collective supply management, or it would be read in Washington as coercion against US policy. Step 3 found that threats failed or backfired whenever the target could replace supply within about 5 years, and Step 4 found that this describes 27 of today's 35 chokepoint rows.

**Sorting rule (from the plan):** "coordinate on" means credible (medium or high) **and** worth having (medium or high safety value). Everything else is "avoid".
